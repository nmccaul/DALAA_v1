# Positioning

*Draft 2026-09-30. Sources: 2026-09-28 meeting, conversations with Nathan.*

## For whom

**Primary:** university instructors who want students to use AI *well*, and who
want evidence of what students actually understand, not just a polished
submission. Starting at BYU.

**Buyer (later):** department chairs, teaching & learning centers, institutions.

## The problem, in their words

- Students use AI for everything; a finished essay no longer proves understanding.
- Existing AI teaching platforms offer dozens of tools that all feel the same.
- Anything new has to work with Canvas, or it won't get used.

## The core message (draft)

> Focused AI learning activities — case discussions, quizzes, explain-it-back —
> that plug into your Canvas course in minutes and show you what your class
> actually thinks.

## The market gap (2×2)

|  | **Prohibit AI** | **Allow AI** |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-technical classes** | AI detectors, proctoring (Turnitin, Honorlock, Proctorio) | **DALAA.** Today's only answer is "report how you used AI"; in reality students let AI do everything |
| **Technical classes** | Proctoring + code-recording / keystroke-replay plugins | AI coding tools (Claude Code, Codex); using them well *is* the skill |

Non-technical classes that allow AI have no real tool. That's the sector we fix.

## What makes it different

1. **Focused tools, not a prompt box.** Each activity is designed around how that
   kind of learning works (case discussion ≠ quiz ≠ explaining).
2. **Class-level insight.** After a case chat, the instructor can put up "52% of
   you took this position," click for anonymous quotes, and run a discussion
   from it. Scott called this "revolutionary" after using it in class.
3. **Built by a professor who uses it.** Tested across multiple semesters in
   real BYU classes, not designed in a vacuum.
4. **Fits Canvas.** Import your course, post grades back; never overwrites a
   grade you changed.
5. **Coaching authoring.** The Case Writer walks instructors through what makes a
   good case instead of dumping a generated draft on them.

## Analogies we use (internal)

- **Office, not Symphony** — separate excellent apps, shared standards.
- **Greatest hits** — a portfolio; not every tool will be a hit.
- **Marriott** — others' tools can join if they meet the standard (e.g., Adam
  Jensen's team-homework quiz tool).
